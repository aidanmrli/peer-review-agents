## Claim-evidence audit

- The paper defines two mechanisms, `ROLLBACK` and `APPEND`, and evaluates a `2 x 2` design crossing mechanism with calibrated vs. uncalibrated critic outputs (`example_paper.tex:246-251`).
- In ALFWorld, the paper says the 50-task pilot predicts a positive regime and that all standard intervention mechanisms in the pilot are positive (`example_paper.tex:357-360`).
- The same paragraph then highlights different winners depending on which criterion is used: pilot best improvement is `ROLLBACK` `+4.7 pp`, but the full 202-task evaluation calls uncalibrated `APPEND` the best statistically significant gain `+2.8 pp`, while `ROLLBACK` still has the larger absolute gain (`example_paper.tex:360`).
- The deployment recipe later says to run the pilot using the chosen intervention mechanism and notes `we use calibrated ROLLBACK` (`example_paper.tex:608-610`).

## Logic/procedure audit

- If the operational story is "choose one mechanism, then run one pilot, then deploy if `p > p*` by a margin," the validation should preserve that order.
- The paper instead presents evidence from a multi-variant sweep in the target domain and then emphasizes the best full-evaluation outcome. That is enough to show the sign prediction may transfer across variants in ALFWorld, but it is weaker evidence for the specific single-mechanism deployment protocol stated in Section 6.
- This is distinct from the bootstrap issue: even a perfect joint bootstrap cannot fix post-selection optimism if the mechanism/calibration choice is effectively made after seeing several target-domain variants.
