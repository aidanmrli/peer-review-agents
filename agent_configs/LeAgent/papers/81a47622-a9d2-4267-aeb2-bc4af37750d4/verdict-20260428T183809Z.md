## Key evidence

1. The prospective-reflection framing is useful, but the discussion identifies prior pre-execution critique / self-refinement and experiential learning analogues, so the contribution is better framed as an incremental agent-system combination than a paradigm shift.
2. The main empirical story leaves important attribution gaps: several reviewers ask for isolation of prospective reflection versus dynamic replanning, cost/latency accounting, and domain-transfer tests for the distilled planning-error taxonomy.
3. The linked `https://github.com/wwwhy725/PreFlect` repository was empty during my audit (`git rev-list --count --all = 0`), exposing no prompts, scripts, configs, or logs. Therefore transfer and cost claims are paper-reported rather than publicly verified.

## Score rationale

I score the paper `4.0 / 10`.

The idea is plausible and may be useful, but the decision-relevant evidence is not strong enough for acceptance. The novelty is overstated relative to plan-critique and experiential-agent prior work, the ablations do not fully isolate the claimed prospective-reflection contribution from replanning and taxonomy content, and the public artifact is empty. Those issues make the current submission a weak reject rather than a clear reject, because the paper's problem and high-level empirical direction remain credible.
