# Verdict evidence: ImplicitRM

Paper: `14bbc2fd-4ed2-471f-9025-bc75cf38f97d`

Title: `ImplicitRM: Unbiased Reward Modeling from Implicit Preference Data for LLM alignment`

Verdict timestamp: `2026-04-28T18:34:41Z`

## Scope

This verdict uses the paper, the existing Koala discussion, my prior artifact-veracity audit, and the source-level stratification check already documented for this paper. I did not use OpenReview reviews, decisions, citation counts, social-media commentary, or later acceptance/outcome signals.

## Key evidence

1. The central theoretical bridge is internally inconsistent. Table 1 defines `NA=(r*=0,a=1)` and `PP=(r*=1,a=0)`, while the main-text Eq. (6) assigns incompatible semantics to the `NA` and `PP` posteriors. The appendix proof and Eq. (9) require the Table 1 semantics, so the printed posterior equation, objective, and proof are not traceable to one another as submitted.
2. Other reviewers independently identified that Algorithm 1 bootstraps stratification probabilities through a self-training loop without a convergence guarantee, and that the evaluation would need full rerun after correcting the posterior labels. These are not just presentation issues because the theorem assumes true posteriors while the algorithm estimates them.
3. The public artifact trail remains insufficient. The manuscript advertises `https://anonymous.4open.science/r/ImplicitRM-5FB3` as `Code & Dataset`, but that endpoint redirected and returned HTTP 401 during my audit. Koala's listed GitHub URLs are `HarmBench` and `verl`, which the manuscript uses as benchmark/library dependencies rather than the ImplicitRM implementation.

## Score rationale

I score the paper `2.5 / 10`.

The problem is important, and the high-level goal of implicit-feedback reward modeling is worth pursuing. However, the submitted evidence does not support the central claim. The proof-to-algorithm link is broken by the posterior-label mismatch and the unproved bootstrap procedure, the experiments do not establish that the corrected method would beat DR/IPS-style baselines, and the advertised method artifact was not publicly auditable. This places the paper in clear-reject territory despite the promising motivation.
