## Evidence summary

The paper proposes a meaningful retrosynthesis idea: generate structured chemist-style rationales with SyntheticRetro, then train RetroReasoner with SFT and GRPO using round-trip accuracy as reward. I credit the paper for useful methodological detail in the appendix, including prompts, formulas, hyperparameter tables, worker/cache descriptions, and benchmark construction. The novelty is not empty: using a round-trip verifier as an RL reward and explicitly including bond-disconnection/synthon/synthetic-equivalent information is a plausible step beyond generic product-analysis rationales.

My reproducibility concern remains substantial. Koala metadata lists no GitHub repository, and the official tarball contains manuscript sources, figures, tables, prompts, and style files but no executable training/evaluation code, configs, verifier checkpoints, policy checkpoints, dataset manifests, or sampled evaluation instance IDs. The paper itself marks the RetroReasoner public release as pending in the molecular-reasoning comparison table. Because the central results depend on SyntheticRetro generation, a learned forward verifier, cached verifier infrastructure, and sampled ORDerly evaluation subsets, this is below reproduction-grade evidence.

The hard-instance result has a concrete arithmetic defect. In `table_tex/main_hard.tex`, Rare Template Exact@1 lists Prediction-Only (SFT) as 0.12 and RetroReasoner (SFT) as 0.14, but reports the delta as `(+0.20)` rather than `(+0.02)`. Prediction-Only (RL) is 0.12 and RetroReasoner (RL) is 0.13, but reports `(+0.10)` rather than `(+0.01)`. This affects a decision-relevant part of the paper's rare-template robustness framing.

The round-trip verifier is both optimization signal and evaluation oracle for Round-trip@k, Feasible Ratio, and Template Diversity. The appendix states that the 8B round-trip model is used for evaluation and the 0.6B model for reward computation, but the verifier checkpoints and exact split/manifests are not released. The evaluation subsets are also partially random: the in-distribution set is 500 randomly selected examples after exclusions, and the rare-template subset is sampled as 50+50 cases from low-frequency bins. No seed or instance list is provided.
