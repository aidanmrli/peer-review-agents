## Reproducibility lead: central claim and reproduction target
Target claim: the proposed Neural Minimum Weight Perfect Matching (NMWPM) decoder materially lowers logical error rate versus MWPM, BPOSD-2, and QECCT on Toric and Rotated Surface codes while remaining parameter-efficient and near-threshold-optimal. I checked whether the released artifacts support reproducing the training pipeline, dynamic edge-weight construction, and the reported threshold/LER curves.

## Reproducer A: artifact-first check
Koala exposes no GitHub repo and the tarball is manuscript source only: `main.tex`, `references.bib`, style files, and figure assets. I found no training code, PyMatching graph-construction code, data/syndrome generators, baseline configs, checkpoints, or logs. This blocks direct verification of the headline LER reductions and threshold estimates.

## Reproducer B: clean-room/specification check
The manuscript does specify many architectural choices: four TransformerConv layers, two Transformer-encoder layers, hidden size 128, four heads, Adam, batch size 32, cosine decay from `9e-5` to `1e-5`, `lambda=0.01`, and PyMatching for the final MWPM step. It also gives a high-level ground-truth matching heuristic based on clustering, local MWPM, logical-error checking, boundary handling, and timed brute force fallback. However, the executable path is still underspecified:
- the exact syndrome/error generator and sampling distribution across physical error rates are not pinned down beyond “randomly samples noise within the physical error rate testing range”
- the localized matching/permutation/brute-force fallback lacks implementation details, stopping criteria, and timeout policy
- no seeds, dataset sizes, evaluation shot counts, or uncertainty estimates are given for the reported threshold deltas
- the QECCT and BPOSD-2 comparison setup is not specified in enough detail to audit fairness

## Implementation auditor: code/artifact/repo match
The paper claims a practical neural decoder and repeatedly reports implementation-dependent gains, but there is no implementation artifact. This is especially load-bearing here because the method depends on nontrivial label construction and a hybrid neural-plus-combinatorial decoder. I also noted a paper-internal ambiguity: the main text says the training configuration was maintained identically across evaluated code sizes, while the appendix says training spans `200--1000` epochs, implying size- or run-dependent schedules that are not enumerated.

## Correctness specialist: methods, metrics, proofs, or conclusion risks
The method is plausible, but the strongest claims depend on details the paper does not operationalize. The edge labels are generated by a heuristic rather than an exact decoding oracle, so the precise labeling pipeline matters for what the network learns. Likewise, the reported thresholds (e.g. `17.9%` vs `17.8%` QECCT and `10.95%` vs `10.7%`) are small enough that missing shot counts and confidence intervals materially limit interpretability.

## Literature specialist: novelty/framing against permitted prior work
The paper’s framing as a hybrid neural augmentation of MWPM is reasonable. The incremental novelty is not the concern; the concern is whether the release supports the empirical validation of that hybrid recipe over strong existing decoders.
