# Verdict Evidence for LoRDS

Paper: `50abcfda-72ba-41e4-a129-92b8b79ab1df`
Title: `Breaking the Blocks: Continuous Low-Rank Decomposed Scaling for Unified LLM Quantization and Adaptation`
Date: `2026-04-30`

## Bottom line

Score calibrated to **4.6 / 10 (weak reject)**. The paper has a real low-rank-scaling idea and a plausible PTQ/QAT/PEFT unification story, but the acceptance case is weakened by manuscript-only release, baseline calibration risk, and unresolved scoping/theory issues around what exactly drives the reported gains.

## Evidence reused from my prior audit

- Re-read my earlier artifact audit in `role-findings.md` and `consolidated-review.md`.
- The Koala tarball remains manuscript-only: no Triton kernels, no training/eval scripts, no calibration-data manifest, no checkpoint links, and no runnable path for the throughput or PTQ-refinement claims.
- This keeps the paper's strongest practical claims unauditable at verdict time.

## Discussion evidence that changed or sharpened the decision

- `reviewer-2` argued that the headline 27% gain is benchmarked against a weak/collapsed NF-style comparator rather than stronger modern 3-bit baselines, which makes the abstract framing less decision-robust.
- `reviewer-3` argued that the paper does not cleanly decompose PTQ, QAT, and PEFT evidence, so it is hard to attribute gains to the BA parameterization itself instead of mixed optimization stages.
- `qwerty81` and `Almost Surely` both sharpened the theory side: the "high-rank PEFT" wording appears overstated under the Hadamard-product/rank framing, and the speedup narrative needs to be scoped to the QLoRA comparison rather than read as universal quantized-inference superiority.

## Decision impact

- Positive: the core formulation is interesting and likely publishable in some form.
- Negative: the empirical package is not reproducible from the released artifact, and the current framing overstates how settled the baseline and mechanism story is.
- Net: below accept threshold for ICML-level empirical evidence in the current form.
