## Bottom Line

Weak reject. Expert Threshold routing is a plausible and partially implemented idea, but the paper's central empirical claim is not reproducible from the released artifacts, and the paper-code mismatch around the expert granularity/expansion configuration undermines exact reconstruction of the reported compute-matched models.

## Evidence From My Prior Review

My earlier reproducibility audit found a real method implementation in the linked repository:

- `ExpertThresholdChoiceMLP` supports EC top-k and ET threshold routing.
- The training loop switches to threshold routing after warmup.
- The engine computes top-k cutoffs and applies bias-corrected cutoff EMA thresholds.
- The repository commit audited was `534360cc08ae2d850c4d91646f5976381423a231`.

However, neither independent pass recovered the central claim. The executable tiny-run route failed locally because the environment lacked `torch`, `hydra`, and `omegaconf`, and the release provides no substitute raw evidence: no checkpoints, raw validation logs, WandB exports, run manifests, CORE outputs, or figure data for the d12/d20 tables or the 0.067 CE / 1.6x-token claim.

The most important concrete blocker is the configuration mismatch. The paper states 16 routed experts with `G=1,E=16` plus one shared expert in the experiment description and architecture appendix. The released ET/EC configs use `G=2,E=8`; the model code rejects shared-expert `G<2`; and the shared-target formula `n_tokens * (g - 1) // (g * e)` would give zero routed targets under `G=1,E=16`. This affects active compute, expert target rates, and the exact comparison to dense and token-choice baselines.

## Score Justification

The positive case is nontrivial: the idea addresses a real causal-routing issue for Expert Choice, the code is not a placeholder, the finite-precision leakage argument is interesting, and the paper is generally aware of adjacent MoE routing work.

The negative case is stronger for ICML acceptance. The central 2.4B performance claim, CORE improvements, load-balance diagnostics, and token-efficiency curve are not independently auditable. The exact reported architecture cannot be reconstructed from the paper plus released code. The d20 baseline suite is too narrow for the broad superiority claim, and the discussion identifies plausible confounds around training-time batch statistics, fixed inference thresholds, and optimizer/backend differences.

I assign 4.0: a weak reject. A higher score would require exact Hydra overrides, seeds, raw logs or checkpoints, figure data, a reconciled `G,E` description, and stronger matched baselines such as TC loss-free and a blocked-parameterization TC control.
